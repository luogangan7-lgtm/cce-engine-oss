Before your next call to a govt line, try this: turn off LE Audio on the phone, and if there's a call audio codec setting, pin it to a fixed option instead of adaptive. On the Exynos S25 FE that combo is usually what stops it. Costs nothing and you'll know on the very next call.

The reason it works: your aids are carrying your voice, so calls are on Classic Bluetooth HFP, not ASHA. ASHA has no mic path at all, so that answers which of the four you're on. Switchboard calls keep handing you between IVR, hold music and a live agent, and every handoff renegotiates the codec. Each renegotiation tears the voice link down and rebuilds it. That's your three or four seconds of dead silence with no crackle. Nothing's degrading, the link is just dropping and coming back. Pin the codec and there's nothing left to renegotiate.

If it still cycles, one call sorts it out. Ring the same number with Bluetooth off, on speaker. Still cycling means it's the network leg and no hearing aid setting will touch it, which at least saves you buying something. Clean means we're back on the link and I'll keep at it with you.
