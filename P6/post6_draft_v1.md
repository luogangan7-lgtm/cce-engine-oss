Right pocket and the left aid drops, left pocket and the right one does. That is not a Bluetooth problem

I build OTC hearing aids in China for other brands, so the radio side is the part I actually know. Nothing for sale here, no links.

Someone in this sub described it better than I could. Phone in the right pocket, the left aid drops out completely. Move it to the left pocket, and now it's the right one. Same phone, same aids, same walk.

I sat with that one a while. Whatever's going wrong swaps sides when the phone swaps sides.

No protocol does that. MFi, ASHA, LE Audio, Classic, none of them know which trouser pocket you used. If this were your phone picking the wrong standard, both aids would suffer together and it wouldn't care where the phone sat. So I think the answer is somewhere else, and it's dumber than any of us want it to be.

2.4 GHz gets absorbed by water. You're mostly water. A phone in your trouser pocket has to push a signal through your hip, your gut and your chest to reach the aid on the far side, while the near one gets an easy ride. That's the whole mechanism. My first guess had been firmware, and I was wrong, and I wish I had something cleverer than this.

Once I started looking for that shape, I kept finding it in this sub.

Outdoors is worse. Indoors some of the signal bounces off walls and furniture and gets around you. Outside there's nothing to bounce off, so the direct path is all you've got, and you're sitting on it.

Holding the phone helps. I've read several people here say the choppiness stops when they carry it in their hand and comes straight back in a pocket. Same distance. Different body in the way.

Walking beats sitting for making it fail. Your arms swing, your torso turns, the path changes twenty times a minute. A link with no margin left drops on every one of those.

And someone here mentioned leaving their phone inside the house, walking twenty feet out, still connected. A wall didn't stop it. A body would have. Drywall is dry. You aren't.

Now my side of the bench, and this is the bit I don't think gets said. These things don't have a real antenna. There's no room. What you get is a folded trace on the flex, or the receiver wire quietly doing double duty as a radiator. On a good day I'm looking at a few dB of gain, and a torso eats far more than a few dB. So they aren't just worse than earbuds at this. They start with almost no margin, which is why they fall over at obstacles a phone to phone link would shrug off.

So try this before anything else, because it's free.

Put the phone in a shirt pocket on the same side as your worst aid. Not a jacket. A shirt, high on the chest. Then walk outside and stream something.

If the dropouts mostly stop, you've got your answer, and the fix is where you carry the phone rather than what you carry.

If they don't, you've still learned something. You now know it isn't the pocket, and the protocol argument is worth having after all.

I'd run that before spending anything. There are people here about to buy a phone over this, and I think some of them are about to spend a thousand dollars on something a shirt pocket fixes for nothing.

Two limits I should be straight about. Android users get this too, so I wouldn't read it as an iPhone story. Someone here had it on a Moto with Oticons. And switching phones does sometimes genuinely help, just not for the reason people give. Different radios run different transmit power and different connection settings, and that's real. It just isn't what the word protocol is doing in most of these threads.

If you try the shirt pocket, say what happened either way. I want the failures more than the successes. A failure tells me my read is wrong, and I'd rather find that out here than keep repeating it.
