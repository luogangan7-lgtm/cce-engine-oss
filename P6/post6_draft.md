Right pocket and the left aid drops, left pocket and the right one does. Try a shirt pocket before you buy a new phone

I build OTC hearing aids in China for other brands, so the radio side is the part I actually know. No links, nothing for sale.

If your aids cut out with the phone in your pocket, there's a test that takes two minutes and costs nothing, and I'd run it before spending a cent on a new phone. Shirt pocket, high on the chest, same side as your worst aid. Not a jacket. Then walk outside and stream something.

Here's why I'd start there rather than with the protocol.

Someone in this sub described the thing better than I could. Phone in the right pocket, the left aid drops out completely. Move it to the left pocket, and now it's the right one. Same phone, same aids, same walk.

I sat with that a while. Whatever's going wrong swaps sides when the phone swaps sides.

No protocol does that. MFi, ASHA, LE Audio, Classic, none of them know which trouser pocket you used. If this were your phone picking the wrong standard, both aids would suffer together and it wouldn't care where the phone sat. My own first guess had been firmware, and I was wrong, and the real answer is dumber than I wanted it to be.

2.4 GHz gets absorbed by water. You're mostly water. A phone in your trouser pocket has to push a signal through your hip, your gut and your chest to reach the aid on the far side, while the near one gets an easy ride. That's it. I've got nothing cleverer than that.

See whether yours fits the shape. I count four versions of it in this sub.

Outdoors is worse than indoors. Inside, some of the signal bounces off walls and furniture and gets around you. Outside there's nothing to bounce off, so the direct path is all you've got, and you're standing on it.

Holding the phone helps. Several people here say the choppiness stops in their hand and comes straight back in a pocket. Same distance. Different body in the way.

Walking is worse than sitting. Your arms swing, your torso turns, the path changes twenty times a minute. A link with no margin left drops on every one of those.

And someone here left their phone in the house, walked twenty feet out, still connected. A wall didn't stop it. A body would have. Drywall is dry. You aren't.

One thing from my side of the bench that I don't think gets said. These don't have a real antenna. There's no room for one. What you get is a folded trace on the flex, or the receiver wire quietly doing double duty as a radiator. On a good day I'm looking at a few dB, and a torso eats far more than that. So they aren't merely worse than earbuds here. They start with almost nothing spare, which is why they fall over at obstacles a phone to phone link would shrug off.

So if the shirt pocket fixes it, the fix is where you carry the phone, not what you carry.

If it doesn't, you've still learned something, and I'd want to know. It would mean the pocket isn't your problem and the protocol argument is worth having after all.

Two limits I should be straight about. Android users get this too, so I wouldn't read it as an iPhone story. Someone here had it on a Moto with Oticons. And switching phones does sometimes genuinely help, just not for the reason people give. Different radios run different transmit power and different connection settings, and that's real. It just isn't what the word protocol is doing in most of these threads.

Either way, say what happened. I want the failures more than the successes. A failure tells me my read is wrong, and I'd rather hear that here than keep repeating it.
