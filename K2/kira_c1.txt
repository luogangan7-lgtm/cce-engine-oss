I pulled our repair notes before answering this, because I wanted to check rather than guess.

Wax guards come up more than anything else, and always the same way. People change them late. By the time an aid sounds dead the wax has usually reached the receiver, and I had to have it explained to me that the receiver isn't a filter, it's the speaker. A guard costs pennies. That part doesn't.

The drying box surprised me. I assumed humidity, but what I kept reading was corrosion on the battery contacts and around the mic ports, and sweat does as much of that as weather. So the box is most worth using in the months people put it away.

I'm not on the bench myself. This is just what the reports keep saying.
