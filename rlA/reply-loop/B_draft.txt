Classic Bluetooth, HFP. That is which of the four you are on, and it is forced: ASHA has no microphone path at all, so the moment your aids carry your voice, the call has left ASHA. Your media may still be on it. Two protocols inside one call.

The symptom. Mic and output dying together, clean silence with no crackle, back in three to four seconds, then cycling, is a link teardown and re-establish. Audio that degrades sounds bad on the way down. Audio that vanishes cleanly is a link that dropped.

The switchboard detail is the useful part. Those calls renegotiate the codec mid call as the far end hands you between IVR, hold music and an agent. Your phone rebuilds the SCO link each time, and on the Exynos S25 FE that rebuild takes longer than the gap allows.

Three tests, in order:
1. A four minute call to an ordinary mobile. If it never drops, the trigger is the call path, not your aids.
2. The same switchboard call with Bluetooth off, on speaker. If it still cycles, it is the network, not the link.
3. Developer options: turn off LE Audio if that toggle exists, and set phone call audio codec to a fixed option instead of adaptive.
